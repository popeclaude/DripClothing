Brand system for Drip, the Dublin-born fashion retailer that puts style within everyone's reach. This document covers the logo, colour, typography, values and voice.

Guidelines v2.0, 2026

The refresh

Drip started in Dublin in 2008 and now trades in over 40 countries. The original mark, a heavy serif wordmark with a droplet beside it, served the brand well but reads as two separate ideas. The refresh merges them into one.

DRIP

The 2008 mark. Droplet and wordmark compete for attention, and the heavy serif has dated.

drıp

The 2026 mark. Lowercase, condensed, and the droplet becomes the dot of the i. One idea, one shape, instantly ownable.

Logo

The wordmark is set in Anton, lowercase, with the droplet replacing the dot of the i. The droplet alone works as the app icon, favicon and garment label.

Primary. Black on white, the default in print and on product.

Reverse. White on Hydro Blue for storefronts, bags and campaign covers.

Icon. The droplet on its own for app icon, favicon and embroidery.

Clearspace and minimum size

  • Keep clearspace around the wordmark equal to the height of the droplet on all sides.
  • Minimum width for the wordmark is 24 mm in print and 80 px on screen.
  • Below minimum size, use the droplet icon instead.

Never

  • Never set the wordmark in uppercase or another typeface.
  • Never detach, recolour or restyle the droplet independently of the wordmark.
  • Never place the mark on busy photography without a solid colour holding shape.
  • Never add outlines, shadows or gradients.

Colour

Black and white do the everyday work. Hydro Blue is the signature: it owns the water reference and separates Drip from the sea of monochrome streetwear brands. Heat and Vapor are campaign accents, used one at a time and never together.

Proportion

Roughly 55 white, 25 black, 15 Hydro Blue, 5 campaign accent. If a layout feels loud, remove the accent before removing the blue.

Typography

Two families, clearly separated jobs. Anton shouts; Archivo explains. Anton is only ever lowercase in the logo, but headlines set in it run uppercase, tight and left aligned, like a poster pasted to a wall.

Headline / AntonFree delivery over 40 euro
Body / Archivo RegularArchivo carries product descriptions, editorial and interface copy. It is open, legible at small sizes, and stays out of the way of the clothes. Sentence case throughout, ragged right, line length under 70 characters.
Detail / Archivo MediumCare labels, size guides, legal and footer text set in Archivo Medium at 12 px or above.

Brand values

Four values, written to be used. If a decision, a product or a campaign contradicts one of these, it does not ship.

Style for everyone

Drip exists so that looking good never depends on income. Every price point is a design decision, not a compromise. If a piece only works at a premium price, it is not a Drip piece.

Move at street speed

Trends start on phones and footpaths, not in seasonal calendars. Drip watches, reacts and ships in weeks. Being early matters more than being polished.

Born outside, not upstairs

The brand takes its cues from the people wearing it: creators, crowds and city streets from Dublin to Seoul. Drip amplifies its audience's taste rather than dictating its own.

Easy everywhere

Online, in-app or on the high street, getting the fit should take minutes. One basket, one account, same price, whichever door the customer walks through.

Voice

Drip talks like a sharp friend with good taste: quick, confident, a bit playful, never trying too hard. Slang is welcome when it is current; forced slang ages faster than the clothes.

Sounds like Drip

  • "New drop Friday. Set an alarm."
  • "Your size is back. Move fast."
  • "Fits from 12 euro. No catch."

Does not sound like Drip

  • "We are delighted to announce our latest collection."
  • "Luxury aesthetics at an accessible price point."
  • "Hey fam, this fit is bussin, no cap!"
